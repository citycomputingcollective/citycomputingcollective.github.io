About the Collective

A shared home for computational urban research.

City Computing Collective connects urban theory, spatial data, computational methods, local knowledge, and public questions.

Community before platform

Governance, moderation, and infrastructure grow with participation, and every change is documented in the public repository.

Why this community exists

City computing is interdisciplinary. Its community should be too.

Relevant work is often fragmented across urban science, GIS, planning, computer science, AI, engineering, public health, economics, design, and local practice. The Collective creates shared pathways between those communities.

Mission

Connect fragmented communities

Bring researchers, students, practitioners, public institutions, and open-source contributors into sustained collaboration.

Practice

Turn conversation into artifacts

Encourage documented data, code, methods, projects, papers, tools, benchmarks, and city-facing outputs.

Values

Be rigorous and responsible

Make assumptions, limitations, provenance, uncertainty, credit, and affected communities visible.

Governance

Principles.

Open participationAnyone acting in good faith may read, ask, share, and contribute. Participation does not require an institutional affiliation.
Transparent moderationRules, category changes, moderation practices, and major community decisions are documented.
Respect and safetyHarassment, discrimination, doxxing, spam, deceptive promotion, and misuse of private information are not accepted.
Research integrityDo not misrepresent evidence, authorship, affiliations, data rights, project status, model performance, or uncertainty.
Community ownershipContributors receive visible credit, and shared infrastructure should gradually avoid dependence on one individual.

How this site works

A website on top of public discussions.

Conversations happen in GitHub Discussions. This website shows a copy of them that is easier to browse, and adds nothing that is not already public there.

What is copiedTitle, link, category, author, activity, a short excerpt, research themes, and a few form answers such as project stage, deadline, or event date.
When it refreshesWhenever a discussion or comment changes, and every six hours. The export runs as a GitHub Action in the public repository.
People directoryIntroductions appear in the directory only when the author ticks the directory box. Otherwise only the title and link are shown.
SubscriptionsRSS for all discussions (feeds/all.xml) and for each research theme, and an iCalendar feed of events (feeds/events.ics). They refresh with the site.
Changing or removing a postEdit or delete it on GitHub. The website follows at the next refresh.
